# DG-013 — Product Strategy Decision Pack

## Purpose

Convert Mizan's product vision into a decision-ready commercial strategy without prematurely locking implementation.

This pack proposes the initial strategic choices. It does not approve them.

## S1 — Initial problem

### Working proposal
Solve **financial management friction + uncertainty** for individuals who have financial information but struggle to maintain a coherent, trustworthy picture of what is happening and what to do next.

### Why
This problem is broader and more defensible than "track expenses" and naturally connects capture, understanding, AI, forecasting, and action.

### Trade-off
A broader problem increases product ambition and requires a carefully bounded initial wedge.

## S2 — Initial product wedge

### Working proposal
**Trust + effortless capture + immediate understanding.**

The first compelling experience should make it dramatically easier to turn real-world financial events into trusted financial records and immediately explain their effect.

### Why
It connects the strongest future differentiators without requiring the full future platform.

### Trade-off
The first release may have fewer visible "advanced AI" features than a demo-first product, but it establishes the data foundation required for useful intelligence.

## S3 — Minimum trustworthy product loop

### Working proposal

**Capture → Interpret → Validate → Commit → Explain**

Where:
- Capture accepts structured input and is designed for future evidence channels.
- Interpret may use deterministic parsing and/or AI.
- Validate is authoritative domain logic.
- Commit creates authoritative financial state.
- Explain shows the user the resulting financial effect and supporting history.

### Trade-off
This is more foundation-heavy than building a dashboard first, but it prevents AI/UI shortcuts from becoming the financial architecture.

## S4 — Early AI scope

### Include early
- natural-language financial entry;
- structured extraction from user input;
- candidate transaction classification;
- ambiguity detection;
- explanations grounded in authoritative financial data.

### Defer until trusted data accumulates
- strong forecasting;
- personalized recommendations;
- scenario optimization;
- proactive financial planning;
- high-impact automation.

### Why
AI quality depends on reliable structured financial context.

### Trade-off
Later AI may be more valuable and safer, but early AI still demonstrates the product's core interaction advantage.

## S5 — Product experience direction

### Working proposal
Mizan should evolve toward a **Financial Inbox + Financial Copilot** experience rather than a dashboard-first application.

Primary surfaces:
1. Financial Inbox — things Mizan received, inferred, detected, or needs confirmation for.
2. Financial Timeline — authoritative history.
3. Financial State — current balances and position.
4. Explain — why something changed.
5. Copilot — natural-language questions and proposals.
6. Insights — proactive findings.
7. Actions — approved plans, reminders, and automation.

### Trade-off
A dashboard remains useful, but it should be a view of the system rather than the product's conceptual center.

## S6 — Monetization hypothesis

### Working proposal
Use a **trust-first freemium subscription model**.

Free/Foundation should establish trust and habit.

Paid tiers should monetize recurring high-value capabilities such as:
- advanced AI capture;
- receipt/voice intelligence;
- deeper analytics;
- forecasting;
- personalized intelligence;
- scenario analysis;
- automation;
- higher usage limits.

### Principle
Do not monetize by selling or exploiting user financial data.

### Trade-off
A free tier may increase acquisition cost, but it can reduce trust barriers for a sensitive financial product.

Pricing and packaging remain validation questions.

## S7 — Product moat hypothesis

The moat should not depend on one AI model or one integration.

Candidate compounding advantages:
- trusted longitudinal financial model;
- high-quality financial history;
- personalized understanding;
- user-approved preferences and workflows;
- explainability and trust;
- evidence normalization;
- domain intelligence;
- automation built on explicit policies.

### Trade-off
These advantages compound over time and are harder to demonstrate in an early demo than isolated AI features.

## S8 — Competitive posture

### Working proposal
Do not compete feature-for-feature with established products.

Compete around the coherent workflow:

**Anything can enter → Mizan understands it → financial truth is maintained → the user understands the effect → Mizan anticipates what matters → the user can act.**

Competitor research remains evidence for validation, not an instruction to clone features.

## S9 — Globalization

### Decision proposal
Global product foundation from inception.

Egypt is the starting validation market/context, not the product boundary.

Localization belongs at explicit edges:
- currency;
- locale;
- language;
- institutions;
- integrations;
- regulatory requirements;
- market-specific workflows.

The core financial semantics should remain stable wherever possible.

## S10 — Strategic expansion order

### Working sequence

**Stage 1 — Trust**
Financial core, history, balances, corrections, auditability.

**Stage 2 — Frictionless Capture**
Natural language, receipt/image, voice, evidence inbox.

**Stage 3 — Understanding**
Categories, recurring items, counterparties, reimbursements, obligations, cash-flow understanding.

**Stage 4 — Intelligence**
Explanations, anomalies, forecasting, affordability, scenarios, recommendations.

**Stage 5 — Action**
Plans, reminders, rules, approved automation.

**Stage 6 — Expansion**
Family/shared finance, freelancer/professional variants, small business, multi-currency, global integrations and ecosystem.

This is a strategic sequence, not a promise to implement every stage before validating the previous one.

## S11 — Validation gates

Before significant expansion, require evidence for:
- users completing trusted setup;
- repeated capture usage;
- measurable reduction in capture effort;
- useful AI proposal acceptance;
- low harmful classification/correction rates;
- users returning for explanations/insights;
- measurable time or decision value;
- willingness to pay;
- absence of unacceptable trust/privacy failures.

## S12 — Product success definition

Mizan should be considered strategically successful when it demonstrates that users repeatedly rely on it to understand and manage their financial position, while the product maintains trustworthy financial truth and creates enough recurring value to support sustainable paid usage.

## Decisions requiring Product Owner approval

1. Accept the initial problem hypothesis.
2. Accept the initial wedge.
3. Accept the minimum trustworthy loop.
4. Accept the early/deferred AI boundary.
5. Accept the Financial Inbox + Copilot experience direction.
6. Accept the trust-first freemium subscription hypothesis.
7. Accept global-by-design as a product decision.
8. Accept the staged expansion sequence.
