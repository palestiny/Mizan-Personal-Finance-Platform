# Mizan — Product Strategy & Market Opportunity

## Status
**State:** Strategic direction / hypotheses to validate
**Phase:** M0 — Product & Domain Foundation

This document translates the vision into a commercial strategy. Strategic hypotheses are not presented as validated market facts.

## 1. Strategic thesis
Mizan should compete as a **Personal Financial Operating System**, not another expense tracker.

The opportunity is the combination of trusted financial state, frictionless capture, richer economic understanding, AI-assisted interpretation, proactive intelligence, and controlled automation.

The product should solve a recurring problem strongly enough that customers perceive the service as worth paying for.

## 2. Target problem
Financial information is fragmented across memory, bank apps, cash, wallets, messages, receipts, notes, spreadsheets, and existing finance applications.

Even when information exists, the user may still lack a coherent answer to:

**What is happening with my money, what does it mean, and what should I do next?**

Mizan's opportunity is to turn fragmented evidence into a trusted financial model and then turn that model into useful decisions and actions.

## 3. Initial wedge
The initial product should prove:
1. Mizan represents financial truth correctly.
2. Mizan makes capture materially easier than manual bookkeeping.
3. Mizan explains financial state in genuinely useful ways.
4. Users experience recurring value that can justify payment.

The initial wedge is therefore **trust + effortless capture + immediate understanding**, not maximum feature count.

## 4. Differentiation hypotheses
- **H1 — Capture without friction:** say, scan, import, or enter what happened.
- **H2 — Understand economic meaning:** represent reimbursements, advances, shared expenses, and payments on behalf of others.
- **H3 — Explain important numbers:** balances, changes, forecasts, and recommendations trace to data and assumptions.
- **H4 — AI on trusted financial state:** AI is useful because it has structured financial context, not because it is merely a chatbot.
- **H5 — Proactive value:** surface relevant information before the user asks.
- **H6 — Controlled action:** consequential changes respect authorization, validation, and audit rules.
- **H7 — Global core:** one financial core supports markets through explicit localization and integration boundaries.

## 5. AI strategy
AI is a capability layer, not the financial source of truth.

AI should be used for:
- natural-language understanding;
- extraction;
- classification;
- ambiguity resolution;
- summarization;
- anomaly detection;
- forecasting assistance;
- scenario generation;
- personalized explanations;
- recommendation generation;
- workflow preparation;
- approved automation.

AI must not silently decide authoritative balances, historical truth, irreversible financial facts, or consequential actions outside explicit policy.

Intended pipeline:

**User/Evidence → AI Interpretation → Structured Proposal → Domain Validation → Confirmation/Policy → Authoritative Financial State → AI Analysis**

This keeps AI replaceable and prevents model behavior from becoming a hidden financial rule.

## 6. Product value ladder
1. **Record** — help me record what happened.
2. **Understand** — tell me what happened.
3. **Explain** — tell me why my position changed.
4. **Anticipate** — tell me what may happen next.
5. **Decide** — help evaluate a financial decision.
6. **Act** — prepare or perform an approved workflow.

The subscription proposition strengthens as Mizan moves from recording toward recurring understanding, anticipation, decision support, and controlled action.

## 7. Commercial model hypothesis
Potential packaging:
- **Free/Foundation:** core records, accounts, history, basic insights, export.
- **Plus:** advanced capture, receipt/voice intelligence, analytics, recurring detection, forecasts, alerts, richer explanations.
- **Intelligence/Pro:** advanced AI analysis, scenario planning, personalized recommendations, automation, higher usage limits.
- **Family:** shared financial spaces, permissions, household workflows, collaborative intelligence.
- **Future professional products:** freelancer, creator, contractor, or small-business variants.

Pricing, packaging, willingness-to-pay, and feature boundaries require validation.

## 8. Revenue principles
Prefer a user-paid model where feasible. User data should not become the commodity that funds the business.

Commercial mechanisms must not compromise privacy, ownership, portability, auditability, correctness, or user control.

Possible future revenue sources include subscriptions, higher-value AI/automation tiers, family plans, professional variants, and carefully governed integrations or partner services.

## 9. Competitive strategy
Do not win by copying every feature of established products.

Build a coherent workflow:

**Capture anything → understand what it means → maintain trusted financial truth → explain the state → anticipate what matters → help the user act.**

Competitor features are market evidence, not the roadmap by themselves.

## 10. Validation strategy
Validate with measurable evidence such as:
- activation and first trusted setup;
- capture effort versus baseline;
- classification correction rate;
- time from evidence to accepted record;
- AI proposal acceptance/edit/rejection rate;
- explanation usefulness;
- forecast usefulness;
- recurring usage and retention;
- conversion to paid and paid retention;
- time saved;
- support/error rate;
- financial correction rate;
- trust incidents.

No single metric should override financial correctness and user trust.

## 11. Strategic non-goals
Mizan should not become:
- a generic chatbot with a finance theme;
- an uncontrolled autonomous financial agent;
- an ad/data-extraction business;
- a country-specific bookkeeping system;
- a feature-copying clone;
- a premature enterprise accounting platform;
- an accounting abstraction detached from user needs.

## 12. Strategic decision gate
Before leaving M0, explicitly decide:
1. first user/problem hypothesis;
2. first differentiated product wedge;
3. minimum trustworthy product loop;
4. AI capabilities for early architecture versus later releases;
5. evidence required before scope expansion;
6. monetization hypothesis to test.

These remain explicit and revisable as evidence changes.
